Durham Region Business Resources for Fall 2026: 5 Digital Moves to Make Now

Published October 8, 2026 · Saleel

Durham Region has new and ongoing resources for entrepreneurs. The real opportunity is turning that support into a stronger digital presence and a clearer growth plan.

Small businesses remain central to Durham Region’s economy. According to the Region’s 2024 Business Count, 71 per cent of local businesses employ between one and 10 people. For owners in Oshawa, Whitby, Ajax, Pickering, Clarington and north Durham, that means growth often depends on making smart use of limited time, money and staff.

Several local resources highlighted in 2026 can help. The Business Advisory Centre Durham (BACD) offers free advisory services, specialized programs and more than 200 workshops each year. Its 360° Growth Program was launched to help established local businesses scale, while Invest Durham maintains funding resources and a regional business directory. Here is how to pair those opportunities with practical digital improvements.

1. Prepare a one-page digital growth plan

Before joining a program or meeting an advisor, define what online growth should produce. Choose one primary business result—more quote requests, appointments, store visits or online sales—and record your current monthly total. Then list the website pages, search terms and marketing channels that influence it.

This simple baseline makes advice more useful. Instead of asking how to “improve marketing,” you can ask how to increase qualified website inquiries from Durham Region by a measurable amount over the next quarter.

2. Audit your local search presence

Check whether your business name, phone number, address and hours are consistent across your website, Google Business Profile and credible directories. Review your service categories, photos and customer reviews. Search for your main service from a phone and note what appears before your business.

The Durham Region Business Directory is another relevant place to review or establish your local presence. A consistent footprint helps customers verify that your business is active and serves their area.

Local visibility is not about being listed everywhere. Focus on accurate information, trusted platforms and a website that clearly explains what you do and where you work.

3. Build a funding-ready project outline

Funding programs change, and eligibility varies. Instead of waiting for the perfect grant, outline a useful project now. State the business problem, the proposed digital improvement, its estimated cost, the timeline and the result you expect to measure.

For example, replacing a slow website could be tied to faster mobile load times, more completed contact forms and lower advertising waste. Invest Durham’s funding resources page can help owners find current opportunities, but a clear project brief makes it easier to decide which ones genuinely fit.

4. Turn workshops into published improvements

Training only creates value when something changes afterward. For every workshop or advisory session, choose one action that can be completed within seven days. Update a service page, add a frequently asked question, install conversion tracking or request reviews from recent customers.

BACD’s workshops and advisory services can provide direction. Your follow-through should create a visible asset or measurable process. Keep a short record of what changed, when it changed and which metric you will review.

5. Make your website ready for growth

A campaign, directory listing or networking event may send new people to your website. Make sure the destination is ready. Each important page should load quickly on mobile, identify the service and service area, answer common questions, show credible proof and offer one obvious next step.

Test every form, booking link and phone number. Confirm that analytics count completed inquiries rather than just page views. If you plan to expand into another Durham community, create genuinely useful location content instead of duplicating the same page with a different city name.

Use local support with a specific objective

Durham Region entrepreneurs do not need to tackle every digital channel at once. Start with a defined business outcome, use local resources to sharpen the plan, and complete a small number of high-impact improvements. That approach turns business news into practical progress—and gives your next marketing decision a stronger foundation.
