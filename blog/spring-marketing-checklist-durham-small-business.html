Spring Marketing Checklist: 10 Things Every Durham Region Small Business Should Do in April

Published on April 2, 2026 by Saleel

Spring is the unofficial fresh start for small businesses across Durham Region. The snow is melting, foot traffic is picking up, and customers are searching for services they put off all winter. Whether you run a landscaping company in Pickering, a salon in Whitby, or a restaurant in Oshawa, April is the month to tighten up your marketing before the busy season hits.

Here are ten practical things you can do right now to make sure your business is ready to capture every opportunity this spring and summer.

1. Audit Your Website for Broken Links and Outdated Info

When was the last time you actually clicked through your own website? Check every page. Make sure your hours are current, your phone number is correct, and your service descriptions still match what you offer. If you added a winter-only promotion banner, take it down. Stale websites signal to customers — and to Google — that nobody is home.

2. Update Your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing people see when they search for your business. Log in and update your spring hours, add fresh photos of your storefront or recent work, and post a seasonal update. Google rewards active profiles with better visibility in local search results. If you have not posted anything since last year, now is the time.

3. Refresh Your Homepage Copy

Your homepage should speak to what customers need right now, not six months ago. A roofing company can highlight spring inspection specials. A personal trainer can promote outdoor bootcamp sessions. Even a small change to your headline or call-to-action can make your site feel current and relevant.

4. Check Your Site Speed on Mobile

More than 60 percent of local searches happen on phones. Run your website through Google PageSpeed Insights and see how it performs on mobile. If your pages take longer than three seconds to load, you are losing visitors before they even see what you offer. Common fixes include compressing images, removing unnecessary plugins, and switching to faster hosting. These are simple changes that can have an outsized impact on conversions.

5. Publish a Spring-Themed Blog Post or Update

Seasonal content is one of the easiest ways to attract local search traffic. Think about the questions your customers ask every spring and turn them into blog posts. "How to prepare your lawn for spring in Durham Region" or "Spring cleaning tips from a professional organizer in Ajax" — these are searches that real people are making right now. A single well-written post can bring steady traffic for weeks.

6. Collect and Respond to Recent Reviews

Spring is a great time to ask satisfied customers for online reviews. Send a follow-up email or text after completing a job and include a direct link to your Google review page. Just as important: respond to every review you have received recently, including negative ones. Thoughtful responses show potential customers that you care about the experience.

7. Plan Your Social Media for the Next 90 Days

You do not need to post every day, but you should have a rough plan. Map out two to three posts per week through June. Mix in behind-the-scenes content, customer spotlights, seasonal tips, and promotions. Batch-creating content in one sitting saves time and prevents those weeks where your social accounts go silent. Tools like Canva and Later make this manageable even without a marketing team.

8. Clean Up Your Email List

If you have an email list, spring-clean it. Remove bounced addresses, re-engage subscribers who have not opened an email in six months with a "still interested?" message, and segment your list if you have not already. A smaller, engaged email list outperforms a large, ignored one every time. Then send a spring update — new services, seasonal deals, or simply a reminder that you exist.

9. Claim and Update Local Directory Listings

Beyond Google, make sure your business information is consistent on Yelp, Yellow Pages, Bing Places, and any industry-specific directories. Inconsistent NAP data (name, address, phone number) confuses search engines and can hurt your local ranking. A quick audit across the major directories takes less than an hour and pays dividends all year.

10. Set a Marketing Budget for Q2

Even a modest budget makes a difference when it is spent intentionally. Decide how much you can allocate to marketing over the next three months and prioritize based on what moves the needle most. For most Durham Region small businesses, that means investing in your website, local SEO, and Google Ads before anything else. Social media ads can supplement, but your owned channels — your website and email list — should come first.

Make This the Spring You Get Ahead

None of these steps require a massive budget or a marketing degree. What they require is intention. The businesses that win in Durham Region are the ones that show up consistently — with a fast website, an active online presence, and a willingness to meet customers where they are searching.
