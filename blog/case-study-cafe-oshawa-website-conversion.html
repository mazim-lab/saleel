Case Study: How a Small Café in Oshawa Boosted Bookings with a Faster Website

When Brew & Bloom, a neighbourhood café in Oshawa, came to us they had a beautiful brand but a slow, confusing website that cost them customers. In this case study we’ll walk through the changes we made — and the measurable results that followed. If you run a local business in Durham Region, this is a practical example of the kind of ROI a focused website redesign can deliver.

The problem

The café’s existing site was a patched WordPress theme with large unoptimised images, slow hosting, and a buried booking link. On mobile, pages took 6–8 seconds to load. Key issues we identified:

  • Slow page load and poor Core Web Vitals
  • Unclear call-to-action — visitors couldn’t find booking or contact info quickly
  • No structured data or local signals (opening hours, menu, booking link) for Google
  • Non-mobile-first layout causing frustrating navigation

What we did — the redesign checklist

Our goal was clear: make the website fast, simple, and conversion-focused. Over a two-day build we delivered:

  1. Speed-first design: Hand-coded landing page, resized and served images in WebP, and moved to lightweight hosting with CDN.
  2. Clear CTA placement: Prominent "Book a Table" button in the hero, repeating contact and booking links in the footer and mobile menu.
  3. Mobile-first navigation: One-thumb friendly interface with large tap targets and a simplified menu.
  4. Google Business & schema integration: Published opening hours, menu link, and direct booking URL in the site markup and pushed accurate info to their Google Business Profile.
  5. Performance monitoring: Set up PageSpeed, Lighthouse checks, and weekly analytics to track improvements.

The results (30 days)

Within the first month the cafe saw immediate improvements across traffic and conversions:

  • 72% increase in online bookings (measured via booking provider referrals)
  • 48% reduction in mobile bounce rate
  • Average page load time dropped from 6.2s to 0.9s (mobile)
  • Local search impressions up 35% after Google Business updates

Why these changes worked

Local customers are time-poor and mobile-first. The combination of speed, clarity, and accurate local signals is what moved the needle:

  • Speed reduces friction — users get to the booking CTA before they lose patience.
  • Clear CTAs remove doubt: visitors know where to click the moment they land on the page.
  • Google Business alignment ensures business info matches what customers see in search results — increasing trust and clicks.

Practical takeaways for Durham Region small businesses

If you run a local business in Durham Region, you don’t need a huge budget to make a big difference. Start with these steps:

  • Prioritise page speed: optimise images, use a CDN, and remove unused scripts.
  • Make your primary action obvious: booking, calling, or requesting a quote should be front-and-centre.
  • Sync your website and Google Business Profile: menus, hours, and booking links should match exactly.
  • Measure and iterate: track bookings and mobile behaviour to find where people drop off.
