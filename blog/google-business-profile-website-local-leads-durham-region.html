How to Use Your Google Business Profile and Website Together to Win More Local Leads

Published July 30, 2026 · Saleel

Local search starts on Google. Someone checks reviews, clicks through, and decides whether to contact you. If they do not work together, you lose leads.

Your Google Business Profile is the front door. Your website is the room behind it where people decide whether to stay. A strong profile gets attention. When they are aligned, customers move from search to action.

That matters because local buyers rarely make decisions in a straight line. A homeowner in Oshawa may check your hours, skim a few reviews, land on your service page, then compare you with two other businesses before calling. A retailer in Whitby may look for pickup options, prices, and recent photos before making a move. If your profile and website tell different stories, the customer feels the mismatch.

1. Keep the basics identical everywhere

Start with consistency. Your business name, phone number, hours, service area, and main services should match on your Google Business Profile and your website. Small differences create confusion for both people and search engines. If your profile says you serve Ajax and Pickering but your website only mentions Durham Region in general, you are leaving room for doubt.

This is not glamorous work, but it is high value. Consistent details help Google trust your business and help customers trust that they are looking at the right company.

2. Send profile visitors to the right page

Too many businesses link their Google Business Profile straight to the homepage and call it a day. That is a missed opportunity. If someone clicks after searching for a specific service, send them to the most relevant page instead.

For example, a plumbing company should link the profile to its emergency service page if that is the main search intent. A web design firm should send traffic to a page that explains services, pricing, and examples of work. The closer the landing page matches the search, the more likely the visitor is to stay.

The shortest path from search to lead is usually not your homepage. It is the page that answers the exact question the customer already has.

3. Use your website to prove what the profile promises

Your profile can say you are professional, local, and responsive. Your website should prove it. That means real project photos, clear service descriptions, strong testimonials, and a visible process. If your Google listing makes you look active but your site feels thin or outdated, the customer senses a disconnect.

Think of your site as the evidence folder. It backs up the claims your profile makes. If you mention same-day response, show it on the site. If you work in a specific neighbourhood, mention it on the page.

4. Make it easy to act on mobile

Most local searches happen on phones. That means the profile and the website both need to feel effortless on a small screen. A tap-to-call button should work. Forms should be short. Hours should be easy to find. Service details should not be buried under a wall of text.

Mobile users are impatient for a reason. They are often searching while busy, on the move, or comparing options quickly. If the profile is clean but the site is slow, the lead slips away before you ever hear from them.

5. Treat reviews like content, not just proof

Reviews do more than provide social proof. They give you language. The words customers use in reviews often reveal what matters most to them: speed, friendliness, reliability, convenience, or pricing. That language should influence your website copy.

If people keep praising your quick turnaround, use that in your headlines. If they mention easy communication, make that part of your service promise. A website that echoes real customer language feels more believable than one that sounds polished but generic.

6. Keep posting with a purpose

Google Business Profile posts are not just filler. Use them to promote seasonal offers, recent projects, new services, and blog posts that answer common questions. Then link those posts to the most relevant page on your site.

If you are posting about a spring maintenance package, send people to a service page that explains what is included. If you are sharing a new case study, link to the full article. Every post should have a next step.

7. Measure the handoff

You do not need fancy reporting to know whether this is working. Watch a few simple signals each month:

  • How many profile views turn into website visits.
  • Which pages get the most traffic from local search.
  • How many calls, form submissions, or bookings come from those visits.
  • Whether visitors bounce quickly or keep exploring.
  • Which services get the most attention after a profile click.

If the profile gets interest but the website does not convert it, the problem is usually the message, the page layout, or the call to action. If the website converts well but the profile gets little attention, the issue may be photos, reviews, or incomplete business info. The fix is rarely one thing. It is usually the connection between the two.

For Durham Region businesses, the best local marketing setup is simple: Google Business Profile brings people in, the website answers their questions, and the two work together without friction. That is how a search turns into a call instead of a missed opportunity.
