Google Business Profile Holiday Checklist for Durham Region Small Businesses

Published November 5, 2026 · Saleel

Holiday shoppers make quick decisions. A few timely Google Business Profile updates can help them see accurate hours, useful offers, and the right next step before they visit or call.

As the holiday season approaches, customers across Oshawa, Whitby, Ajax, Pickering, Clarington, and the rest of Durham Region will search for nearby products and services with immediate intent. They want to know who is open, what is available, and whether a business can solve their problem today.

Your Google Business Profile often provides that information before someone reaches your website. An outdated listing can create a wasted trip or missed call. An accurate, active profile makes it easier for a customer to choose you. Use this checklist before seasonal demand gets busy.

1. Add special holiday hours

Regular business hours do not automatically explain closures, shortened days, or extended shopping hours. Add special hours for every date that differs from your normal schedule, including days when you are closed. Google can then show customers that the hours were confirmed instead of warning that they “might differ.”

Review the schedule again whenever staffing or weather changes. If your business is appointment-only during part of the season, explain that in a post and on the relevant website page.

2. Confirm every contact detail

Check your phone number, website link, address, service area, booking link, and business category. Call the listed number from another phone and test every link on mobile. A small typo or broken booking page can quietly lose high-intent customers.

Service-area businesses should list the communities they genuinely serve without adding virtual offices or locations where they are not staffed. Consistent, honest details help both customers and Google understand the business.

3. Publish a useful seasonal update

Create a Google Business Profile post that answers one timely question. Announce an order deadline, gift-card option, seasonal service, holiday menu, extended hours, or booking cutoff. Keep the message specific and link to the exact page where the customer can act.

A useful holiday post answers four things quickly: what is available, who it is for, when the deadline is, and where to buy, book, or learn more.

4. Refresh photos with current information

Add recent, well-lit photos of your storefront, team, products, completed work, or seasonal setup. Customers use images to judge whether a business looks active and credible. If directions are confusing, upload an exterior photo that helps first-time visitors recognize the entrance or parking area.

Avoid graphics packed with tiny text. Profile photos appear at different sizes, especially on phones, so the main subject should be clear at a glance.

5. Check products, services, and menus

Remove unavailable items and update descriptions, pricing, and links where appropriate. Highlight seasonal services or popular gift options, but do not promise inventory you cannot maintain. Restaurants should confirm that menus linked from the profile match what customers can currently order.

Your website should carry the full details. Use the profile to help customers discover the offer, then send them to a focused landing page with availability, terms, pickup or delivery information, and a clear call to action.

6. Prepare for questions and reviews

Holiday demand can produce more messages, calls, questions, and reviews. Decide who will monitor the profile and how quickly they should respond. Build short, accurate answers for recurring questions about deadlines, returns, accessibility, service areas, and pickup.

Ask satisfied customers for honest reviews using your direct review link, but never offer incentives or filter requests based on expected ratings. Reply professionally to both positive and negative feedback. Useful responses show future customers that the business is attentive.

7. Track the actions that matter

Record a baseline before the busy period, then review calls, website clicks, direction requests, bookings, and other profile interactions. Add campaign parameters to website links when possible so analytics can identify traffic from Google Business Profile.

Do not judge success only by profile views. The better question is whether more people completed a valuable action. After the season, note which posts, photos, offers, and landing pages generated results. That record will make next year’s planning faster and more evidence-based.

A holiday-ready profile is not a one-time promotion. It is a reliable source of local business information connected to a website that helps customers take the next step. Set aside 30 minutes now, assign someone to monitor changes, and keep the details current throughout the season.
